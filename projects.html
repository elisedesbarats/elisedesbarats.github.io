Projects

My doctoral work aims to increase our understanding of how emotional responses to music change neural representations of pain

Thesis work

Neural mediators of pain relief by music

Collaboration with the Computational Cognitive Affective Neuroscience Laboratory of Sungkyunkwan University, South Korea

  • Study design, data collection and analysis for a large scale two-site fMRI study investigating pain relief by favourite music
  • Analyses of behavioural and brain data included multilevel models, multivariate pattern expression, ROI and wholebrain mediation using R, python and MATLAB
  • Co-authored a CIHR awarded project grant

Behavioural mediators of pain relief by music

  • Conducted a series of multilevel linear models to investigate how emotional responses to musical features predict pain relief
  • Demonstrated that high depth and relaxing music was best for pain, but that this effect was mediated by pleasure/displeasure and not physiological arousal

Role of emotion and distraction in pain relief by music

  • Investigated whether emotional responses to music add to pain relief beyond attentional distraction using a dual-task paradigm
  • Frequenist and bayesian analyses in R quantifying interaction effects
  • Showed that music can reduce pain even when attention is recruited by a cognitive task

Coauthored contributions

I work in a multidisciplinary team of music therapists, music researchers, and pain neuroscientists to create consensus based resources for research
