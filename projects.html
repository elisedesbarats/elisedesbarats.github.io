Thesis

My doctoral work aims to increase our understanding of how emotional responses to music change neural representations of pain.

Neural mediators of pain relief by music

Ongoing collaboration with the Computational Cognitive Affective Neuroscience Laboratory of Sungkyunkwan University, South Korea

    • fMRI study design — data collection and analysis for a large scale two-site study investigating pain relief by favourite music
    • Multilevel modelling — analyses included multivariate pattern expression, ROI and wholebrain mediation using R, Python and MATLAB
    • Grant writing — co-authored a CIHR awarded project grant

Behavioural mediators of pain relief by music

Desbarats, E., Valevicius, D., Lépine Lopez, A., Vogel, T.A., Greenberg, D.M., Zatorre, R.J., Roy, M. Perceived pleasantness mediates the effects of high-arousal and emotionally complex music on pain. Under review.

  • Multilevel mediation — conducted a series of models to test how emotional responses to musical features predict pain relief
  • Key finding — high depth and relaxing music was best for pain, but this effect was mediated by pleasure/displeasure and not physiological arousal

Role of emotion and distraction in pain relief by music

Ongoing collaboration with the Music4Pain network

  • Dual-task paradigm — investigated whether emotional responses to music add to pain relief beyond attentional distraction
  • Frequentist & Bayesian analyses — quantified interaction effects in R
  • Key finding — music can reduce pain even when attention is recruited by a cognitive task
